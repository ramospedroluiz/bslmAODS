# Illustrative comparison: concrete compressive strength

The original analysis decisions were recorded on 9 September 2026, after an API/runtime pilot and before inspecting out-of-fold predictive scores. Subsequent diagnostic amendments are recorded chronologically below. This is a local analysis protocol, not a registered study.

## Data and prediction target

UCI Concrete Compressive Strength, I-Cheng Yeh (1998), DOI 10.24432/C5PK67, CC BY 4.0. Retain all 1,030 observations, including 25 repeated full rows. Predict strength (MPa) for a held-out reported composition, using seven ingredients and log(age). All ages and repetitions of an identical seven-ingredient composition remain in one of five folds. There are 428 compositions. Folds are balanced on counts without using strength. Recipe grouping does not identify laboratory batches or near-duplicate compositions.

Standardize all eight inputs and the response using training-fold means and standard deviations only. Fit on 824 observations and predict the other 206 in each fold. Return forecasts to MPa before scoring. Do not tune on test responses. This is not a comparison of optimally tuned algorithms or a claim of universal ranking.

## Models

* Bayesian ridge, Bayesian lasso, horseshoe: bayesreg Gaussian likelihood, package hierarchical priors, four independently seeded chains, initially 2,000 warm-up plus 2,000 retained draws per chain, thinning one.
* Bayesian model averaging: BAS fixed g=n Gaussian linear models, uniform prior on all 256 subsets of eight inputs, flat common intercept and scale prior as implemented by BAS. Integrate coefficients, residual scale and model membership using the native Student-t mixtures.
* BART: the original dbarts::bart specification used 200 trees, depth parameters 0.95 and 2, k=2, 100 uniformly spaced candidate cuts, inverse-chi-squared residual variance prior with three degrees of freedom and 0.9 calibration quantile. Package training-data calibration of leaf and residual scales is retained and disclosed. Four chains with the same initial budget as above. The later 1,000-tree specification and its continuation budget are documented in the second amendment below.
* GAM: mgcv Gaussian additive cubic regression splines, k=10 per input, REML, select=TRUE. Use the coefficient covariance corrected for smoothing-parameter uncertainty (unconditional=TRUE); residual scale remains a plug-in estimate. Also retain the uncorrected covariance as a sensitivity analysis.
* GP: DiceKriging, constant trend, product Matern-5/2 covariance with separate input ranges, estimated nugget, three likelihood-optimization starts, range bounds [0.1,20] on standardized inputs, maximum 150 optimizer iterations. Conditional Gaussian prediction with universal-kriging trend uncertainty; native predictive variance already contains the nugget.
* Neural network: brnn, eight tanh hidden units, fixed architecture, training standardization supplied externally, evidence/Gauss-Newton regularization, up to 3,000 epochs. Construct a local Gaussian parameter covariance from 2*beta*J'J+2*alpha*I and linearize the predictor. Add 1/(2*beta) residual variance. Alpha and beta are fixed at their evidence estimates. This adapter is neither MCMC nor exact Bayesian inference.
* Reference: ordinary least squares on the same eight inputs, conventional Student-t predictive distribution with n-p-1 degrees of freedom. This row is a classical reference, not an additional Bayesian fit.

## Diagnostics and sensitivity

For sampled fits, calculate rank-normalized split R-hat and bulk/tail ESS for residual standard deviation, every held-out latent prediction, and (for linear models) coefficients and log global variance. If maximum R-hat exceeds 1.01 or either minimum ESS is below 400, refit with four times the retained and warm-up budget, using new recorded seeds. If this also fails, allow one further doubling. Report the full history and any remaining diagnostic failures. Test responses are not used for this decision. Independently seeded chains use package initialization; they are not claimed to have deliberately overdispersed starts.

Report optimizer status, GP bounds, GAM convergence and network stopping reason. Evaluate BART k=1 and k=3 on fold 1 selected in advance; retain k=2 as the primary analysis regardless of sensitivity results. Compare corrected and conditional GAM covariance on every fold. These are limited sensitivity checks, not exhaustive prior robustness assessment.

## Evaluation

Pool one held-out prediction per observation. Report RMSE of predictive means, MAE of predictive medians, negative log predictive density (NLPD), CRPS, central 90% coverage and width. Inspect central 50%, 80%, 90% and 95% coverage and probability integral transforms (PIT). For normal/Student-t mixtures, evaluate density and CDF from mixture components; draw 8,000 posterior-predictive responses for empirical CRPS and predictive quantiles. Single Gaussian or Student-t forecasts use analytic intervals and CRPS. Record CRPS Monte Carlo sensitivity using the first/second halves of predictive draws. Do not equate conditional posterior intervals with empirically calibrated intervals.

Use 2,000 composition-level bootstrap resamples of the fixed out-of-fold forecasts for descriptive intervals and paired differences. These intervals condition on the folds and fitted models; they do not include variability from refitting, fold selection, or acquisition of a new dataset. The resampling unit is composition and the estimand remains observation-weighted. No test-set recalibration.

Measure elapsed time for fitting (all four chains and any diagnostic extensions for the reported specification), and separately for construction/scoring of the held-out predictive distribution. Record historical specifications and exploratory diagnostic pilots separately. Run models sequentially, with one core requested and BLAS/OpenMP thread settings set to one. Timings describe this machine and implementation, not hardware-independent complexity. Preserve package versions, source/data hashes, folds, seeds, session information, diagnostic histories and row-level predictions. Exclude package binaries from the shareable supplement.

## First diagnostic amendment, before original predictive scoring

After inspecting the original BART chain diagnostics, and still before inspecting out-of-fold predictive scores, the 200-tree BART fits were excluded from the primary score table, calibration figure and paired comparisons. Those computed finite mixtures remain in the supplement explicitly labeled diagnostic_only. This was a reporting decision based on unresolved numerical accuracy, not a claim that BART has inferior predictive performance or a change selected using test scores. At that stage, it left seven Bayesian or empirical-Bayesian predictive procedures and the OLS reference in the primary comparison. The original BART fitting settings, costs, unsuccessful diagnostic extensions, and chainwise output are reported separately.

Before predictive scoring, a complementary BMA prior-sensitivity check was added using fixed g=n/10 and g=10n on all five folds. All 256 subsets are enumerated under each setting. This check permits prior effects to be assessed without chain-mixing ambiguity; it does not replace the primary g=n specification. Its addition was motivated by the BART diagnostic difficulties, not by held-out predictive performance.

On fold 1, BART::wbart produced maximum R-hat 1.505 with 2,000 retained draws per chain and 1.412 with 8,000, with minimum bulk ESS below 9. The attempted 16,000-draw extension was interrupted. These diagnostics concern held-out latent function values, not held-out responses or scores. The original BART implementation was changed to dbarts::bart, retaining the stated model settings but using its default tree proposal mixture. The original fitting log is retained as pilot history. No predictive ranking was inspected to make this change. All original primary fits were rerun with the then-final source hashes, and preliminary computational effort was recorded separately from primary fit timings. DiceKriging does not retain an optimizer convergence code in the km object; numerical warnings, likelihood, fitted ranges and boundary checks are recorded rather than asserting a retained code of zero.

## Second diagnostic amendment: retaining BART in the illustration

After the original results had been examined, a further diagnostic investigation was undertaken on the same data and folds. No dataset or fold seed was changed. The aim was to obtain a usable BART posterior approximation, not to select a favorable held-out score. It would therefore be incorrect to describe the entire revised analysis as specified before any predictive results were seen. No new held-out response scores were calculated to select the revised tree specification.

On fold 1, three four-chain diagnostic pilots used 4,000 warm-up and 2,000 retained iterations per chain: 200 trees with training-quantile cuts, 1,000 trees with the original uniform cuts, and 50 trees with training-quantile cuts. The maximum R-hat values were 1.4554, 1.0407, and 2.4802, respectively; minimum bulk ESS values were 8.12, 87.25, and 4.84. The 1,000-tree specification was selected for extended fitting from these diagnostics, not from test-response prediction errors. Increasing the tree count changes the prior over functions even though the package scales each tree's leaf prior with the ensemble size; it is not merely a longer run of the original posterior target. No prior-effect estimate is inferred by comparing this fit with an inadequately mixed 200-tree fit.

The revised BART specification fixes 1,000 trees, k=2, depth parameters 0.95 and 2, and the original 100 uniform candidate cuts. The residual prior and training-data calibration are unchanged. For each of the five folds, run four independently seeded chains with 8,000 warm-up and initially 16,000 retained iterations each. If any monitored R-hat exceeds 1.01 or either minimum bulk or tail ESS is below 400, continue the same chains and RNG states to 32,000 and, if necessary, 64,000 retained iterations per chain. Check residual standard deviation and all 206 held-out latent means. Calculate new held-out response scores only after all five folds meet the unchanged thresholds. These diagnostics are necessary checks on the monitored summaries, not a proof of global posterior exploration.

The 1,000-tree forecasts are identified as BART-1000 in the machine-readable results and BART in the article. They enter the nine-procedure primary comparison only after the diagnostic gate passes. Earlier 200-tree and k-sensitivity forecasts remain diagnostic_only. All eight non-tree primary fits and the BMA and GAM sensitivity calculations are retained unchanged. Fitting time for the revised BART row includes its four chains, warm-up and any continuation; unsuccessful historical specifications and the three new pilots are documented separately rather than charged to the reproducible run of the final specification.

The revised sampler uses the installed dbarts 0.9-34 continuation interface. An implementation test verifies that an uninterrupted chain and an in-memory segmented chain produce identical draws; a serialized and reloaded continuation agrees within 1e-12 with the same RNG state. The execution environment for the revised BART fits is recorded separately because the same package versions were restored from the lockfile between the original comparison and this amendment.
